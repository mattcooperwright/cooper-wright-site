At IDEO, project teams run a set of rituals we call Flights. A Pre-Flight happens at kick-off, a Mid-Flight once the work has found its altitude, and a Post-Flight when it lands. The last one is the easiest to skip when the next project is already pulling at you, so I wanted to write down what it covers. Thanks to Abby Detrich, PMP for the nudge :)

We run this in a shared document so that everyone can add notes. We use Gogole Slides, but Figma, Miro, Mural etc would work too. Even Google Docs, or Powerpoint

The Post-Flight is a structured hour or so of looking back together. It opens with a quick check-in on how everyone is arriving, then asks each person for one word to describe the project. From there the team talks through highs and lows: where the work reached real creative excellence, and where it struggled and why. Then it turns to learning. The first set of questions is personal. What will you take to future projects, and what pushed you to grow or show up in new ways?

**The second set is for IDEO, and it's my favourite part:**

- What would you tell a future team about this client?
- What did we learn that should change how we scope work like this?
- What do you wish we could have done, inside the scope or outside it?

The session closes on gratitude, and on one last open question: is there anything we haven't talked about that we should have?

Two design choices make it work. It deliberately isn't a space for 1:1 feedback (have other rituals for that), which lets people talk about their own experience without it turning into a review.

Ideally the learnings don't stay with the team. Each completed deck is synthesised anonymously alongside other projects' Flights, so what one team learns about scoping or client dynamics can shape how the next one starts. Although I will admit that our system for this could be much stronger.

All of this process is definitely influenced by a past era of design projects with a begining, middle, and end. It's less obvious for a team involved in continuous delivery, but I see no reason for any creative team to pause and ask these questions periodally.

After all, its human to want phases of work with moments of closure.

I believe.
